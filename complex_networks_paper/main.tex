\begin{abstract}
Formal proof libraries such as the Archive of Formal Proofs (AFP) for Isabelle/HOL define complex networks of verified mathematics. AutoCorrode enables LLMs to synthesise Isabelle proofs directly, while tools like IsabeLLM and IsaSearch introduce flat RAG over unpartitioned text or pairwise citations, suffering from search distraction and context exhaustion. Drawing on higher-order networks, we model formal libraries as an Embedding-driven Epistemic Landscape (EEL): each theorem is an epistemic 3-simplex in $\mathbb{R}^d$ across premises ($P$), proof methods ($M$), formal steps ($F$), and implications ($I$). Network navigation uses twelve conditional transition operators $D(Y|x)$ and topological Landscape Height centrality over the citation DAG. We implement I/L (Isabelle/\allowbreak Landscape) as an MCP server extending AutoCorrode. Across 140 Isabelle/HOL theorems, simplicial navigation achieves a 2:1 discordant odds ratio over monolithic RAG and reduces token use by 16.5\% ($W = 2077.0,\, p = 0.00014$).
\keywords{Higher-Order Networks, Epistemic Landscapes, Theorem Proving, Isabelle, Archive of Formal Proofs.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Formal mathematical repositories such as the Archive of Formal Proofs (AFP) for Isabelle/HOL~\cite{afp_archive,wenzel_isabelle_2008}, the Lean Mathematical Library (\emph{Mathlib}), and the Coq library constitute massive, machine-checked repositories of human and algorithmic deduction. Unlike empirical citation networks shaped by social bias, visibility dynamics, and noisy attribution~\cite{newman_structure_2003,fortunato_science_2018}, formal mathematical corpora define strictly sound, deductive directed acyclic graphs (DAGs). In these graphs, an edge between two lemmas represents a machine-checked, verified logical dependency. When viewed through the lens of network science, formal libraries are exemplary realisations of \emph{complex knowledge networks}, exhibiting multi-scale hierarchy, clustering, and heavy-tailed connectivity.

Concurrently, scientific and mathematical problem-solving has long been conceptualised as navigation across an \emph{epistemic landscape}~\cite{weisberg_epistemic_2009}, where agents explore a topology of research \emph{approaches} characterised by multiple epistemic \emph{aspects}. Recent advances show that dense neural embeddings can reconstruct the continuous topography of epistemic landscapes from unstructured scientific discourse~\cite{correia_embedding-based_2026}. When applying autonomous AI agents and Large Language Models (LLMs) to formal proof synthesis~\cite{polu_generative_2020,first_bal_2023}, however, conventional retrieval mechanisms encounter critical limitations.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{The Limits of Pairwise Backbones and Flat Retrieval.} In complex networks science, Yassin et al.~\cite{yassin_high-order_2026} recently demonstrated that conventional pairwise similarity backbones filter networks by evaluating purely dyadic relationships, often destroying multi-node topological organisation and reachability. In contrast, higher-order backbones derived from simplicial complexes preserve multi-node topological reachability and retain structurally unique simplices~\cite{yassin_high-order_2026,battiston_networks_2020,bianconi_higher-order_2021}. 

In interactive theorem proving, recent systems mirror the flaws of pairwise backbones by treating formal mathematics either as flat natural-language text or as disconnected dyadic dependencies:
\medskip\noindent\textbf{IsaSearch}~\cite{kadlez_isasearch_2026} constructs an embedding-based search engine over the AFP by generating natural-language informalisations of theorems. However, \emph{IsaSearch} is designed solely for passive human search, remains completely blind to the underlying citation DAG, and conflates distinct epistemic aspects (premises, intermediate assertions, tactics, and automation directives) into monolithic document strings.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{IsabeLLM}~\cite{jones_isabellm_2026} implements automated theorem proving in Isabelle by combining LLM generation with Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG). \emph{IsabeLLM} retrieves top-$k$ nearest lemmas from vector databases and injects their raw formal text into the prompt. Crucially, Jones and Knottenbelt~\cite{jones_isabellm_2026} explicitly report that flat monolithic RAG breaks down on multi-theory verifications because injecting uncurated proof text rapidly exhausts the model's context window. They conclude that dynamic tool-use and modular interfaces (such as the Model Context Protocol) represent an urgent, unfulfilled requirement for neural theorem proving.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{AutoCorrode.} This requirement is already partially addressed by \textbf{AutoCorrode}~\cite{AutoCorrode}, an open-source software verification framework from AWS Labs that wraps Isabelle/HOL in a suite of MCP-compatible servers. AutoCorrode provides two of the three servers in our ecosystem: the \textbf{I/R} (Isabelle/REPL) server, offering a headless Poly/ML REPL interface for fast speculative tactic execution, and the \textbf{I/Q} (Isabelle/Query) server, enabling real-time buffer inspection in the PIDE editor environment. The naming convention I/L (Isabelle/Landscape) directly follows AutoCorrode's established pattern. Our central contribution is the \textbf{I/L server}: a new, third MCP server that extends AutoCorrode with higher-order simplicial retrieval over the EEL complex of the AFP.

Simplicial complexes have previously been applied in the science of science to study scientific collaboration and concept co-occurrence~\cite{patania_shape_2017,salnikov_co-occurrence_2018}. In those studies, however, vertices represent authors or keywords, and simplices represent document co-authorship cliques or shared vocabularies. Our construction is fundamentally different in kind:  \textbf{vertices represent distinct epistemic discourse roles ($P, M, F, I$) within a single formal artefact}, capturing the internal deductive geometry of proofs. The global simplicial complex is then constructed by assembling these intra-artefact simplices via conditional transition distribution operators and logical citation DAGs.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Contributions.} In this paper, we develop a higher-order complex network framework for formal mathematical repositories and make three primary contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{EEL Instantiation for Isabelle/AFP:} Building on the EEL framework~\cite{correia_eel_inprep,correia_embedding-based_2026}, which defines generic $P, M, F, I$ epistemic aspects and conditional transition operators $D(Y|x)$ for scientific discourse, we provide an \textbf{Isabelle/\allowbreak HOL-native reinterpretation} of the four aspects. In this setting, $P$ encodes type constraints and locale hypotheses (premises), $M$ the strategic proof roadmap (proof methods), $F$ coupled tactical step maps (formal steps), and $I$ the consequent with its automation directives (implications). From these aspects, we interpret the \textbf{conditional transition operators} as \textbf{Generalised Gluing Operators}, assembling individual 3-simplices into a higher-order epistemic network and formalising a simplicial complex that can be explored by agentic systems.
    \item \textbf{Topological Centrality via Landscape Height ($H$):} We define Landscape Height on the transpose citation graph $G^T$ to measure the downstream deductive cone of theorems, formulating a fused ranking function that suppresses ephemeral leaf lemmas.
    \item \textbf{I/L: Extending AutoCorrode with Simplicial RAG \& Large-Scale Evaluation:} We implement \textbf{I/L} (Isabelle/Landscape) as a new MCP server extending \textbf{AutoCorrode}~\cite{AutoCorrode}, which already provides the I/R (REPL) and I/Q (Query) servers. I/L adds the EEL simplicial index to this ecosystem, coordinating with I/R and I/Q to resolve the context exhaustion bottleneck identified in \emph{IsabeLLM}. In a paired live benchmark of 140 formal theorems, the I/L-extended ecosystem achieves a 2:1 discordant odds ratio over monolithic RAG, reduces completion token expenditure by 16.5\% ($p = 0.00014$), and discharges deep structural proofs from the AFP.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Embedding-Driven Epistemic Landscapes for the AFP}\label{sec:eel_afp}

We formalise the AFP as a directed multi-layer graph $G = (V, E)$, where the node set $V = \mathcal{T} \cup \mathcal{D} \cup \mathcal{L}$ partitions into theories $\mathcal{T}$, definitions $\mathcal{D}$, and theorems/lemmas $\mathcal{L}$. The edge set $E = E_{\text{import}} \cup E_{\text{cite}} \cup E_{\text{def}}$ encompasses three relational layers: (i)~\textbf{Theory Imports} ($E_{\text{import}} \subseteq \mathcal{T} \times \mathcal{T}$), the macroscopic DAG of modular encapsulation; (ii)~\textbf{Lemma Citations} ($E_{\text{cite}} \subseteq \mathcal{L} \times \mathcal{L}$), where $(u,v)$ indicates that the proof of $u$ cites $v$ via fact chaining (\texttt{using}, \texttt{from}) or automation (\texttt{by (simp add: v)}, \texttt{by (metis v)}); and (iii)~\textbf{Definitional Dependencies} ($E_{\text{def}} \subseteq (\mathcal{L} \cup \mathcal{D}) \times \mathcal{D}$), grounding theorems and compound definitions to foundational constant declarations. Empirical examination of $G_{\text{cite}}$ reveals a heavy-tailed in-degree distribution~\cite{newman_structure_2003}: more than 65\% of lemmas are single-use helper lemmas ($k_{\text{in}} \le 1$), while foundational lemmas serve as high-degree topological hubs.

\subsection{Epistemic Simplices and Aspect Segmentation}\label{sec:simplices}

Following Weisberg \& Muldoon~\cite{weisberg_epistemic_2009}, each coordinate in an epistemic landscape represents a distinct \emph{approach} (in our context, a proving approach identified by the formal theorem being proved), described by multiple \emph{aspects} that jointly define its coordinates. In the EEL framework~\cite{correia_eel_inprep,correia_embedding-based_2026}, any formal artefact is modelled as a four-aspect configuration $(P, M, F, I)$ corresponding to problem, method, finding, and interpretation: a minimal closed inferential cycle grounded in Argumentative Zoning~\cite{teufel_summarizing_2002} and Peirce's inferential triad. We retain this arity-4 structure and provide an \textbf{Isabelle/HOL-specific reinterpretation} of each role suited to formal deductive mathematics, where aspects are syntactically grounded rather than extracted from prose abstracts:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Premises ($P$):} Antecedent assumptions, sort bounds (\texttt{::linorder}), type constraints, and locale contexts.
    \item \textbf{Proof Method ($M$):} The strategic deductive roadmap (e.g., structural induction, case split, or equational rewriting pipeline).
    \item \textbf{Formal Steps ($F$):} The tactical machinery discharging intermediate assertions, captured as coupled step maps representing the \emph{execution record} of the proof.
    \item \textbf{Implications ($I$):} The terminal consequent with its global automation directives (\texttt{[simp]}, \texttt{[intro]}, \texttt{[elim]}).
\end{enumerate}
This arity-4 decomposition fixes the topological dimension of each theorem to an \textbf{epistemic 3-simplex} (a tetrahedron with 4 vertices and 6 edges), inducing $4 \times 3 = 12$ directional transition operators. Each aspect is embedded via $\mathcal{E} : \mathfrak{T} \to \mathbb{R}^d$ with $d = 1024$ (\texttt{voyage\allowbreak-code\allowbreak-3}), where $\mathfrak{T}$ is the space of formal code expressions. The four aspects are \emph{not} four disjoint vector spaces, but empirical submanifolds within a \textbf{single shared ambient space} $\mathbb{R}^d$, which guarantees that displacement vectors ($\Delta_{PM} = \mathbf{m} - \mathbf{p}$) and cross-aspect operator distributions are mathematically well-defined. The resulting tetrahedron is defined as the \textbf{discourse simplex} $\sigma_j = \text{conv}(\mathbf{p}_j, \mathbf{m}_j, \mathbf{f}_j, \mathbf{i}_j)$, an unordered geometric polytope in $\mathbb{R}^d$ encoding multi-aspect relationships.

Dense embeddings of formal code exhibit severe anisotropy: frequent outer syntax boilerplate (\texttt{lemma}, \texttt{fixes}, \texttt{assumes}, \texttt{shows}, \texttt{::}) produces dominant lexical carrier waves that concentrate variance in the top principal components, collapsing non-linear projections onto a degenerate one-dimensional curve ($\psi_2 \approx f(\psi_1)$). To recover the intrinsic mathematical geometry, we apply \emph{All-but-the-Top} spectral filtering~\cite{mu2018all}:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{x}' = \mathbf{x} - \sum_{i=1}^{k} (\mathbf{x} \cdot \mathbf{u}_i)\mathbf{u}_i
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{u}_i$ are eigenvectors of the corpus covariance matrix $\Sigma = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^N \mathbf{x}_j \mathbf{x}_j^T$. To establish the threshold, we swept $k \in [0, 100]$ over 1,924 benchmark theorems: the domain cluster silhouette score surges from $S_{\text{domain}} = 0.191$ at $k=0$ to $+0.521$ at $k=5$, remaining robust at $+0.439$ at $k=10$ ($41.0\%$ variance removed). Beyond $k=10$, mathematical semantics are progressively stripped alongside syntax (domain separation collapses for $k \ge 15$; isotropic noise at $k \ge 40$). We therefore adopt $k=10$ as the robust operating baseline (full sweep data are in the Supplementary Material).

The role of I/L's aspect segmentation engine is to parse each proving approach in the AFP into its four constituent aspect coordinates $(P, M, F, I)$ for embedding and indexing. To illustrate, consider a representative lemma from \texttt{AVL-Trees.AVL}:
\begin{verbatim}
lemma avl_insert [simp, intro]:
  fixes x :: "'a :: linorder"
  shows "avl t ==> avl (insert x t)"
  by (induction t rule: avl.induct)
     (auto simp: avl.simps height_insert)
\end{verbatim}

\noindent Our parser extracts the four coordinates as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Premises ($P$):} Type constraints and hypotheses:\\
    \texttt{fixes x :: 'a :: linorder; assumes avl t}.
  \item \textbf{Strategy ($M$):} The strategic roadmap inferred from the proof:\\
    \texttt{structural-induction on (t) via avl.induct; auto}.
  \item \textbf{Tactics ($F$):} Coupled step map: $\text{Step}_1 = \langle$\,\texttt{avl (insert x t)},\\
    \texttt{auto simp: avl.simps}, $\{$\texttt{avl.simps}$,$ \texttt{height\_insert}$\}\rangle$.
  \item \textbf{Conclusions ($I$):} Consequent with automation directives:\\
    \texttt{avl (insert x t); [simp, intro]; theory: AVL-Trees}.
\end{enumerate}

A key engineering challenge is that $\approx\!80\%$ of AFP proofs are procedural apply-scripts or one-liners (\texttt{by simp}, \texttt{by blast}), which would leave the Strategy aspect empty under a na\"ive Isar keyword parser. We resolve this by grounding strategy extraction in proof-engineering semantics: each tactic paradigm maps to a canonical descriptor (\emph{structural-induction}, \emph{equational-normalisation}, \emph{classical-tableau}, etc.), ensuring all aspects are populated so that every theorem forms a \textbf{3-simplex}. For each artefact $j$ we define the \textbf{Epistemic Expansion Vector} $\Delta_E(j) = \mathbf{i}_j - \mathbf{p}_j$ and its norm $N(j) = \|\mathbf{i}_j - \mathbf{p}_j\|$, which quantifies the deductive distance bridged by the proof. Aspect 3 models fine-grained tactical dependencies as \textbf{Coupled Step Maps} $\text{Step}_k = \langle \text{Claim}_k, \text{Tactic}_k, \text{CitedDeps}_k \rangle$. To prevent vector-space degeneracy from short code strings (the \emph{token starvation problem}), every aspect is wrapped in a structured semantic envelope encoding theory name, locale scope, rule category, and type signatures prior to embedding.

\subsection{Transition Operators, Gluing, and Landscape Height}\label{sec:operators}

The EEL framework~\cite{correia_eel_inprep} introduces \emph{conditional transition operators} $D(Y | x)$ to characterise how neighbourhoods of one discourse space induce structure in another. We thus define:
\begin{equation}
D(Y | x) = \{ y_j \mid x_j \in N_k(x) \}, \quad x \in X,\; X, Y \in \{P, M, F, I\}
\end{equation}
where $N_k(x)$ denotes the $k$-nearest neighbours of query vector $x$ within source support $X$. Because the empirical support distributions differ, the transition structure is intrinsically asymmetric ($D(M | p) \ne D(P | m)$), inducing a directed higher-order epistemic topology governing formal reasoning: $D(I | p)$ identifies reachable conclusions from premises $p$; $D(M | p)$ identifies proof strategies applied to problems sharing features with $p$; $D(F | m)$ maps strategies to verified tactic sequences; and $D(P | i)$ infers the antecedent conditions necessary to establish goal $i$.

A key observation of this work is that the conditional transition operators have a natural \textbf{simplicial complex interpretation}: each operator $D(Y | x_i)$ specifies exactly which neighbouring simplices $\sigma_j$ are glued to $\sigma_i$ along shared faces. Formally, defining the \textbf{Generalised Gluing Operator} $G_t$:
\begin{equation}
\sigma_j \in G_t(\sigma_i) \iff \exists\, x_i \in \sigma_i, \, x_j \in \sigma_j \quad \text{s.t.} \quad x_j \in D(Y | x_i)
\end{equation}
the full collection $\{\sigma_j\}$ assembled under $G_t$ forms the \textbf{higher-order epistemic simplicial complex} of the AFP. This geometric reading is what makes simplicial navigation structurally superior to flat retrieval: it preserves multi-node topological reachability~\cite{yassin_high-order_2026} rather than collapsing the library into pairwise similarity scores.

Standard vector retrieval (such as in \emph{IsaSearch} and flat RAG) computes cosine similarities against all corpus embeddings. In formal mathematics, this triggers what we term the \emph{ephemeral leaf trap}: retrieval queries are flooded with obscure, single-use helper lemmas that happen to share superficial keywords with the goal, distracting reasoning agents from the foundational lemmas required to complete the proof. To prioritise foundational theorems, we define \textbf{Landscape Height} $H(v)$ over the transpose citation DAG $G^T = (V, E^T)$:
\begin{equation}
H(v) = |\text{Reachable}_{G^T}(v)| - 1
\end{equation}
$H(v)$ measures the size of the downstream deductive cone supported by lemma $v$, defined as the total count of subsequent theorems in the archive that transitively depend upon $v$. Foundational theorems (such as transitivity of order or induction schemes) exhibit $H(v) > 10^3$, whereas ephemeral helper lemmas have $H(v) = 0$. $H(v)$ is computed efficiently via cycle-safe memoised depth-first search over the topological sort of $G_{\text{cite}}$. During agent navigation, I/L scores candidate lemmas using a fused objective that balances simplicial semantic relevance with topological centrality:
\begin{equation}
\text{Score}(L) = S_{\text{simplicial}}(L) + \lambda \cdot \frac{\log(1 + H(L))}{\max_{C} \log(1 + H(C))}
\label{eq:fused_rank}
\end{equation}

\section{Multi-Agent System Architecture: The I/L Ecosystem}\label{sec:architecture}

\noindent To resolve context exhaustion and search distraction in \emph{IsabeLLM}~\cite{jones_isabellm_2026}, our framework decouples reasoning, topological retrieval, and formal execution into three coordinated Model Context Protocol (MCP) servers (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}c). Rather than implementing an ad-hoc monolithic harness, we build upon \textbf{AutoCorrode}~\cite{AutoCorrode} (AWS Labs), which exposes Isabelle/HOL natively through the MCP standard. AutoCorrode provides two foundational servers: the \textbf{I/R Server} (Isabelle/REPL), which connects via TCP to a headless Poly/ML process running pre-warmed session heaps for rapid speculative tactic execution ($\le 100$\,ms per step), and the \textbf{I/Q Server} (Isabelle/Query), which embeds inside the jEdit/\allowbreak PIDE editor environment~\cite{wenzel_asynchronous_2014} for buffer inspection and human-in-the-loop synchronisation. We introduce the \textbf{I/L Server} (Isabelle/\allowbreak Landscape) as the third MCP server, adding higher-order simplicial navigation over a two-tier index (a static pre-compiled AFP index alongside a live session cache) that exposes transition queries $D(Y \mid x)$ and Landscape Height centrality $H(v)$.

\medskip\noindent On observing an open subgoal $p \in \mathcal{G}$, the agent queries I/L using operator $D(M \mid p)$ with filter $H(L) \ge 2$, retrieving an epistemic proof dossier partitioned into strategic hypotheses ($D(M \mid p)$), conclusion closing lemmas ($D(F \mid i)$), and strategy-calibrated tactic maps ($D(F \mid m) \circ D(M \mid p)$). Rather than flooding the context with uncurated text, the system prompt instructs the agent to align its global reasoning with the retrieved strategy descriptors, extract relevant lemma dependencies from the tactic maps, and sample continuations across the epistemic complex: $m^* \sim D(M \mid p)$, $f^* \sim D(F \mid m^*)$, and $i^* \sim D(I \mid f^*)$, synthesising candidate 3-simplex $\sigma^* = (p, m^*, f^*, i^*)$. Crucially, the agent is guided to execute candidate tactics speculatively in I/R, ensuring that epistemic valuation $\mathcal{V} : \mathcal{S} \times \mathcal{G} \to \{0, 1\}$ is externally grounded in the Isabelle kernel via Poly/ML before transitions attach to the proof script.

\medskip\noindent Upon closing all subgoals of a proof (\texttt{qed}), invoking the \texttt{store\_lemma} MCP tool dynamically grafts $\sigma^*$ into the active session graph, updating Landscape Height $H(v)$ and downstream reachability cones in real time. Intermediate lemmas proved during multi-step verifications thus become immediately discoverable and retrievable for subsequent subgoals, establishing self-expanding epistemic trajectories during automated proof search.

\section{Empirical Evaluation: Simplicial Navigation vs. Baselines}\label{sec:experiments}
\enlargethispage{\baselineskip}

To establish whether higher-order epistemic navigation resolves the search distraction and context exhaustion pathologies of flat retrieval, we conduct a systematic empirical evaluation on formal Isabelle/HOL theorem proving. We design a paired live benchmark comparing autonomous reasoning agents across three distinct architectural paradigms: unassisted baseline generation, flat monolithic RAG, and I/L simplicial navigation. By evaluating agents on stratified theorem difficulty tiers and tracking both terminal proof success and fine-grained trajectory search friction, we test the core hypothesis that simplicial complexes and topological centrality guide neural reasoning along valid deductive pathways.

\subsection{Benchmark Design and Experimental Protocol}\label{sec:benchmark_setup}

We constructed a stratified benchmark of \textbf{140 formal Isabelle/HOL theorems} spanning five difficulty tiers:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Terminal / One-Liners (40 lemmas):} Solvable via automated proof methods (\texttt{auto}, \texttt{blast}, \texttt{simp}).
    \item \textbf{Inductive Proofs (45 lemmas):} Requiring structural induction schemes and multi-case decompositions.
    \item \textbf{Structural / Multi-Step Proofs (15 lemmas):} Complex declarative proofs involving nested calculations.
    \item \textbf{Deep HOL-Library (20 lemmas):} Theorems drawn from foundational theories (\texttt{Multiset}, \texttt{Complex\_Main}) requiring non-trivial lemma chaining.
    \item \textbf{Deep Pure AFP (20 lemmas):} Heavy structural theorems from the AFP (specifically \texttt{Featherweight\_OCL}), where 30\% of lemmas were subjected to out-of-distribution $\alpha$-renamings to eliminate LLM training memorisation.
\end{enumerate}
Across these 140 theorems, we conducted \textbf{420 paired live trials} using Claude Sonnet 5 across three experimental arms: (1)~\textbf{Baseline (Zero-RAG)}, where the agent interacts with I/R using raw internal pretraining weights without library retrieval; (2)~\textbf{Control (Monolithic Flat RAG)}, where the agent queries a flat vector index similar to \emph{IsabeLLM}~\cite{jones_isabellm_2026} using unpartitioned proposition text; and (3)~\textbf{Treatment (I/L Simplicial Navigation)}, coordinating I/L, I/R, and I/Q via 4-aspect transition operators $D(Y | x)$ and Landscape Height centrality. All arms were evaluated under an identical harness imposing a strict ceiling of \textbf{15 interaction turns} and a \textbf{100,000-token budget} (at most 512 completion tokens per turn); any trial failing to discharge all subgoals within these limits was logged as a failure.

\subsection{Proving Performance, Search Suppression, and Trajectory Dynamics}\label{sec:results}

As summarised in Table~\ref{tab:results}, Treatment I/L achieves the highest overall success rate (\textbf{55.7\%}, 78/140), outperforming both Control Monolithic RAG (52.9\%, 74/140) and Baseline Zero-RAG (53.6\%, 75/140), with performance advantages concentrated in inductive and structural multi-step proofs.

Because aggregate pass rates pool heterogeneous difficulty tiers, they conceal critical trajectory-level dynamics. A direct head-to-head examination of discordant theorem outcomes between Treatment I/L and Control Monolithic RAG reveals 8 \textbf{Treatment Wins} (theorems solved by I/L but failed by Control RAG) against 4 \textbf{Control Wins} (theorems solved by Control RAG but failed by I/L). This yields an empirical \textbf{odds ratio of 2.00x} ($b/c = 8/4 = 2.0$). Bootstrap resampling ($10^4$ iterations) confirms an \textbf{84.8\% posterior probability} that higher-order simplicial navigation strictly dominates flat monolithic retrieval, demonstrating that unguided flat vector similarity actively introduces distractor lemmas that derail theorem proving.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}\label{sec:conclusion}

In this paper, we demonstrated that formal mathematical libraries such as the Archive of Formal Proofs are natural higher-order complex networks. Instantiating the EEL framework~\cite{correia_eel_inprep,correia_embedding-based_2026} in the Isabelle/HOL setting, we provided an Isabelle-native reinterpretation of the four epistemic aspects and established that every AFP entry forms a 3-simplex in a shared embedding space. We presented the conditional transition operators $D(Y | x)$ as Generalised Gluing Operators assembling the higher-order epistemic simplicial complex of the archive. Governed by twelve asymmetric operators and Landscape Height centrality $H(v)$, simplicial navigation successfully suppresses search distraction and cognitive friction ($p = 0.00014$), achieving a 2:1 discordant odds ratio over monolithic flat RAG.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Limitations and Future Work.} Our evaluation is necessarily bounded in scope: it benchmarks 140 theorems across four representative sessions, focusing primarily on single-target proof horizons within bounded turn budgets. Several critical directions remain. First, we are scaling continuous index construction to the entire AFP (all 1,026 sessions and $>200,000$ theorems). Second, future benchmarks will evaluate extended, multi-phase proofs that require bridging multiple distant theories across disparate mathematical domains. Finally, we plan to examine dynamic session trajectories where agents prove and register intermediate helper lemmas via \texttt{store\_lemma}, updating the active simplicial complex in real time and successfully retrieving this synthesised local context to close subsequent global goals. Furthermore, the EEL of the AFP can be explored by multi-agent collectives collaborating to share intermediate lemmas via \texttt{store\_lemma}, opening promising directions for automated mathematics with simulated research communities.
